EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Soardist USA is a large-format custom print manufacturer serving roughly 2,000 active wholesale customers, with production in Chicago and California and a third facility in China. Canopy tents are the flagship line, running on Durst P5 Textile hardware with LIFT ERP and its bundled Workflow RIP.

The constraint is not demand or capacity. It is the manual layer between the two. Ten to eleven people do nothing but process orders: receiving artwork by email, producing mock-ups, chasing approvals over three-to-five-day cycles, and re-keying order data from Shopify into LIFT by hand. Fourteen designers absorb work customers could largely do themselves.

This proposal removes that layer. Wholesale customers install a branded Shopify app, publish Soardist's catalog at their own markup, and let their customers design canopies in a live 3D tool that produces a production-composed print file at checkout. Orders flow through one integration layer into a production dashboard, with every stage syncing back to the merchant's Shopify order.

Three decisions that define the build

Single ERP connection

A middleware layer means LIFT ERP sees one integration whether Soardist has 20 merchants or 2,000, a flat cost, not one that multiplies.

Production-ready output

The customiser composes four-panel layouts with trim, bleed and fold geometry. The file the operator opens is the file the press accepts.

Private first, then public

Validated on Soardist's own store before App Store submission, so review starts from a proven product.

Because ownership is exclusive, there is nothing to merge. A sync problem is a delivery problem, never a question of which system is right, and the class of bug that comes with two-way conflict resolution does not exist in this design.

Confirmed in Discovery

LIFT is cloud-hosted; its integration surface has not yet been established. ELEMENT 03: FAILURE & RECOVERY ✕

What happens when something breaks

Production cannot stop because an integration is having a bad day. Every case below is a QA test case in Phase 05, exercised against a live LIFT environment.

LIFT unreachable

Work orders queue durably and in order. The floor keeps working in the dashboard; the queue drains on recovery. No job is lost and none is created twice.

Duplicate delivery

Every write carries an idempotency key derived from the Shopify order ID. LIFT receiving the same message twice still produces one work order.

Write rejected by LIFT

Moves to a dead-letter queue with the error attached and is raised in the dashboard for a human. It does not block the jobs behind it.

Stage change while LIFT is down

Accepted in the dashboard and queued. Shopify write-back and end-customer tracking are unaffected. The customer sees no delay.

Payment not settled

No write to LIFT at all. The job holds on the platform and the five-day rule governs its queue place.

Artwork rejected or under 150 dpi

Holds at art check. It never reaches LIFT and never reaches the press.

Systems diverge

Nightly reconciliation diffs job records across both systems and reports mismatches. Nothing is auto-merged and nothing is silently overwritten.

Replay & rollback

Work orders are replayable from the platform record, so LIFT's view of any job can be rebuilt without re-entering data by hand.

Operating principle: the platform degrades toward Soardist's current process, never below it. In the worst case, with LIFT unavailable for an extended period, orders still arrive, artwork is still validated and composed, and the floor still produces from the dashboard. The queue drains when LIFT returns.

PLATFORM SCOPE: WHY LIFT ERP STAYS

An execution layer, not an ERP

The production dashboard looks like an ERP screen, so it is worth being exact about what it is. It is a manufacturing execution layer: it runs jobs through the floor. LIFT is a business system: it runs the company's books, materials and accounts. This engagement replaces the manual layer between Shopify and production, not the ERP underneath it.

The dashboard takes over
  • Order intake from every merchant store, without re-keying
  • Artwork validation, pre-press approval and print-file composition
  • The live job queue across Chicago, California and China
  • Stage control from art check through to shipping
  • Facility load, turnaround and ship-by management
  • Carrier labels, tracking and blind fulfilment
  • Merchant-facing status, invoicing and payment collection
  • Throughput, on-time rate and value reporting
LIFT keeps
  • General ledger, tax and financial reporting
  • Accounts receivable and payable, credit and terms
  • Material purchasing, stock and consumption
  • Job costing and true margin per order
  • Plant calendar and capacity master data
  • Quoting for work that never touches the platform
  • The audit archive of every job predating launch
  • Whatever the Durst pipeline requires of it

Why replacement is not in this engagement

Durst dependency

LIFT ships bundled with the P5 and sits alongside Workflow RIP. Whether the press pipeline can run without it is a vendor question to be answered, not an assumption to build on.

Footprint not yet audited

Nobody can responsibly price the replacement of a system whose functional surface has not been established. That audit is a Phase 01 deliverable of this engagement.

One change at a time

Launching a customer-facing platform and cutting over the ERP together moves two systems of record at once with no fallback. If the platform has a bad week, the business still has to invoice, buy material and pay people.

The calendar

Go-live is deliberately set ahead of the March–June peak. An ERP cutover carries data migration and a fiscal boundary, and does not fit that window at any budget.

How the stages work

  • Observe: through the seven support months the platform runs as the production system of record. What LIFT is still genuinely used for becomes a matter of evidence rather than opinion.
